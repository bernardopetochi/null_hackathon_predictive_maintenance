What is real and what is invented

ItemStatusDetail
~9 bar pressure, ~90–96 °C brew water, ~25–30 s shotconventionCommonly cited espresso brewing targets. Used only as healthy baselines; not measured on a real machine here.
Fault signatures (which signal moves, direction, size)inventedPlausible-sounding but unvalidated physics, written by us: scale slows heat recovery and drops temp; gasket wear drops pressure and shortens shots; failing pump raises vibration and pressure noise; dull burrs shorten and destabilise shot time. Magnitudes are guesses.
Fault lifetimes and wear curvesinventedScale 1500–3000 shots, gasket 800–1600, pump 600–1200, burrs 1000–2000; severity = (elapsed/life)^k. Not from failure statistics.
Sensor noise, machine-to-machine variationinventedGaussian noise, slow random drift, plus non-fault nuisances the model must ignore: barista grind tweaks (shift shot time), a daily room-temperature cycle, and occasional sensor glitch spikes.
100 shots/dayinventedAssumed busy-café volume, only used to convert shots to days.
The model and the evaluationrealA neural net (15→32→5) and an RUL regressor are actually trained in your browser on the simulated fleet and scored on separately simulated machines. Numbers above are measured, but only on our simulator, so they say nothing yet about real machines.
Limitations: one fault at a time; the model knows only the four fault types it was trained on; healthy baseline assumes the first 40 shots are fault-free.
